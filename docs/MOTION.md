# Homepage motion

How the site moves, as built. Code comments point here by section name. The choreography is pure functions of scroll in `src/lib/motion/home.ts` (and `footer.ts`), unit tested in `tests/motion.test.ts`, and how touch screens settle is in `settle.ts` (`tests/settle.test.ts`); the components that drive them are `HomeMotion.tsx` (homepage), `MotionRoot.tsx` (every page), `Preloader.tsx` (the GTA intro), `ScreenTransition.tsx` and `MainNav.tsx`.

The Studio Foundry motion specification Sebra supplied (MOTION-SPEC.md, 22 October 2026) was the early reference for the engine and the section order. The site has since moved away from it in most details (see "Where this differs from the spec"); this file describes the site, not the spec.

## Motion vocabulary

One set of rules for the whole site (`home.ts` header; the CSS side is in `globals.css`, `:root`):

- **Sheets.** A section hands over by a sheet rising from the bottom edge over a layer that recedes: it grows 8% at most, darkens, or dims to 0.72. Timed sheets use `--e-sheet` over 0.6 to 1.0s; scrubbed wipes take about 0.3 screens.
- **Masks.** Display type never fades. Each line rises from its own mask (`MASK`, 120%) and leaves upward through it (`roll()`). Masked lines take 0.9 to 1.2s timed, or 0.24 to 0.34 screens scrubbed, 0.03 to 0.06 screens apart. Only small labels, captions and blocks may fade.
- **Eases.** Entrances `power3Out`, exits `power2In`, no overshoot. Hover and tone changes take 0.3s on `--e-ui`.
- **Rhythm.** Each beat holds 0.6 to 0.8 screens fully composed and hands over in about 0.3. The next section's first element starts entering before the last one leaves, so no frame is empty for more than 0.15 screens.
- **Materials.** The ivory sheet over the hero, stone under Works, ivory for Values, dusk into the night Services (an OKLab blend), the ivory Contact sheet and the charcoal footer.

## Scroll engine

- **Lenis 1.3.27** (`src/lib/motion/engine.ts`) smooths the wheel: duration 1.2 on an exponential ease-out, vertical. With reduced motion: duration 0, linear, no wheel smoothing. Touch-first screens (a coarse pointer and no hover: phones and tablets) run without it and scroll natively, since Lenis's touch listeners cannot be passive: with it, every touch waited for the page's script before the page could move. There the engine reads the scroll position each frame, and while the page is held (the menu, a page change, the preloader) it cancels the gestures itself (`blockPanels`), except over a panel that scrolls by itself.
- **One frame loop** runs everything in a fixed order: Lenis, frame callbacks (roll links), then the modules by `ORDER`: the line splitter, ground, hero, intro, the story, works, values, the Services picture, the Services copy, the Services to Contact handover, contact, the footer, parallax, and the header last. `dt` is clamped to 50ms, so a tab left in the background does not jump when it comes back. Until the intro's map is complete the loop neither measures nor writes (`measureAfter`; see the load sequence).
- **Lines.** Every block of text the homepage reveals line by line is split into masked lines (`split.ts`) before any module measures, all together (`splitAll`): every block's words are written, every break read, every line written, then every new width read, so the first measure lays the page out about eight times in all rather than twice a block (on a phone it had laid it out 52 times, in a frame of a quarter of a second at four times CPU throttle). Values splits its descriptions itself, together, once it has set the width they wrap to. A split is made again only when its block's width changes, or when it was made before the fonts had loaded.
- **Progress values.** `sso` (0 when an element's top meets the bottom of the screen, 1 at the top) and `hso` (−1 when its bottom meets the bottom of the screen, 0 at the top). Ranges come from the `offsetTop` chain, so transforms never feed back into them, and are re-measured on resize, font load, body resize and route change. The screen unit is `100lvh`, so a phone's toolbar does not change the timeline.
- **Writes.** `setStyle` writes only when a value changes, and the modules write transforms, opacity and clip-path, so a frame never invalidates more than the elements that move. The one custom property written per frame (`--ground`, on the phone header bar) is registered as not inherited.
- **Sleep.** Every module is a pure function of the scroll, so one whose section is more than a screen off the screen, above or below, would write the same styles every frame. Each homepage section's module writes its frame once as the section goes that far, then skips its updates until the section comes back within a screen (`sleeper`); a measure wakes it. The page's ground and the Services to Contact handover, which reach past their own sections, always run, as do the footer, the parallax and the header.
- **Layers.** A line or block lifted through its mask is a layer of its own (`translate3d`) only while it moves: at rest it has no transform, and parked under its mask a flat one, so it is drawn with the page. Left as layers, every line ever revealed had stayed one (113 to 198 layers down the page on a phone), which cost memory and every frame's compositing; now there are 54 to 75.
- **Keeping the place.** After a resize or rotation the engine puts the reader back at the same point of the same section.
- **Tracks.** The pinned, scrubbed layouts run only when motion is allowed, JavaScript runs and the screen is at least 500px tall (`TRACKS_QUERY`, the `tracks:` Tailwind variant). Otherwise every section shows a still, ordinary layout with all content visible (see "Reduced motion and still layouts").

## Settle (touch screens)

A flick on a phone ends wherever its momentum runs out, and on the scrubbed scenes that was often in the middle of a change: at 390×844, 39 of the 78 places a thumb's flicks came to rest down the homepage left a paragraph cut by its masks, a word half rolled or a picture half wiped. Where a touch screen is the main way in (`Frame.touch`: no Lenis, a coarse pointer and no hover), the page is never left at rest like that. The wheel and the desktop are unchanged: nothing there settles or runs on its own clock.

- **Where a scene is part way.** Each pinned scene's rules are in `home.ts` ("Touch screens"): a masked line cut by its mask (more than 3% of its height off its place and less than 3% short of hidden, `lineCut`), or a wipe, the ring's fling, the trace turning to paper, the drawing straightening or the dusk begun and not finished (`partWay`). Each module turns them into the spans of scroll where they hold (`zones`), sampled every hundredth of a screen; the engine joins the modules' spans, and any with less than 0.15 screens of rest between them (`SETTLE_JOIN`), the first time the page comes to rest after a measure, so the sampling never costs a frame that moves. Drawings in progress (the story's sketch and engineering, and the making-of drawings' drafts, each stroke a finished line), pictures on the move (the ring, the drifts, the Values pictures' growth) and the sheets that rise with the page between sections are whole at every moment, so the page may stop among them.
- **The glide.** Once the page has been still for 90ms (`SETTLE_WAIT`) with no finger down, inside a change, the engine glides it on to the change's end in the direction it was last moving (`settleTarget`): on down to the next composed moment, or back up to the one before. It takes 0.3s for a short way up to 0.8s for a long one (`settleDuration`), and sets off and arrives at rest (`power2InOut`, `settleAt`), written each frame before the modules draw. It never goes back the way the flick came, or a gentle flick into a change would be undone every time and the page could never be scrolled on. A finger down, a page lock or a new measure stops it, and so does anything else that scrolls the page (a key, a link, focus, the last of a flick's momentum); the page is judged again once it is still.
- **Entrances on their own clock.** Pieces that come up with the page rather than on a pinned stage (Services' group pictures and list on phones, the Services frame's sheet as it comes up, all of Contact) cannot be settled: the page is free to rest anywhere around them. There each plays its entrance on its own clock from the moment the page brings it to its start (`entranceClock`): a masked line over 0.9s on `power3Out`, a block 0.8s, a picture's sheet 1s, the title block's drawing 0.9s on `power2InOut` (its cells following the edge in, 0.15s after it starts and 0.12s more for each cell before it in its row), through the same states as the scrubbed version (`servicesRevealAt`, `contactRiseAt`, `contactPictureAt`). Pieces that start in the same frame go 0.07s apart in reading order, so a block that arrives at once still comes in line by line; one that goes back below the screen is reset, and plays again the next time it comes up.

## Load sequence

The screen on a full page load is the Greater Toronto Area intro (`Preloader.tsx`; timeline in `src/lib/motion/intro.ts`, unit tested in `tests/intro.test.ts`; browser tests in `e2e/intro.spec.ts`). It plays on every full load (Sebra, 24 October 2026: the photos belong in the loader wherever it shows): the head script marks the load with `data-intro`; after the first visit the pictures come from the browser's cache. Moving between pages in the site never reloads, so it uses the page transition instead, which shows the same map with its pictures.

The map is the five census divisions of the GTA from Statistics Canada's 2021 cartographic boundary file, drawn in straight lines with about forty corners, shared edges kept shared, north up (`src/content/gta-map.ts`, generated by `scripts/gta-map/build.py`; see `docs/IMAGES.md`). Each region is an HTML layer sized to its bounding box and clipped to its shape (`clip-path: url(#intro-clip-…)`, a clip path in the box's own units), so its picture fades and settles on the compositor; the white lines are one SVG over all five, drawn with `stroke-dashoffset` on `pathLength=1` paths at a constant width (`vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`), with a soft dark line under them for pale skies.

The drawing is painted on the main thread, so it waits until hydration is over: started at first paint, a phone at four times CPU throttle stalled the first line for up to 180ms. |
| 0 to 0.4s | The dark screen holds. |

| 0.4 to 2.15s | Under the map, the old loader's label, GREATER TORONTO AREA in the mono face, fades up as the outline starts (0.6s), and its red dot (`--color-brand-400`, the old loader's red) pops in as the outline closes (0.45s). |

The hero's CSS entrance moves each line's inner span and the scroll moves the span around it, so neither overrides the other.

Measured with Playwright at 390x844, DPR 3, four times CPU throttle: no frame over 33ms while the lines draw or the pictures come in; the measure's one long frame (about 130ms) falls in the hold.

### The story

How a project moves through the firm, told with one client house (the narrow contemporary one) on one drawing sheet. `e` is screens of scroll since the stage pinned; chapter i owns 1.2 screens from i × 1.2, and the stage releases at 4.8 (`STORY`, `storyState`).

- **Vision** (from −0.9). The sheet wipes up with the stage from the bottom of the screen (0.6 screens, `power3Out`), its shadow kept outside the clip, and the front elevation is sketched on it line by line over 1.12 screens: the ground, the volumes' outlines, the openings, the details, then the joints and boards, each line drawn on `power1InOut` and run a little past its ends, as a hand draws (`storyLineDraw`, `STORY_OVERSHOOT`).
- **Design** (from 1.02). The rendering wipes up from the ground line (0.48 screens, `power2InOut`). The sketch is clipped above the wipe's edge, so every line gives way to the edge it was traced from, and a hairline rides the edge.
- **Engineering** (from 2.24). Trace rises over the picture (0.36 screens), letting 16% of it through, and the engineering is drawn on it (`storyOverlay`): the three grid lines down from their bubbles, the four levels across from their datums, then the beams, posts and footings round their outlines in red, the footings dashed below grade, their labels fading in last.
- **Permit** (from 3.44). The trace turns to paper and the structure goes (0.26 screens), the lines come back in ink (0.22) and straighten from the picture's perspective into a true elevation (0.5, `storySeg`), and the title block's four fields roll in through their masks from 3.86: project, drawing, sheet number, issued for building permit.
- **Text.** Each chapter's label, title lines and body lines rise through their masks on the Works rhythm (`storyLine`): from 0.03 screens before the chapter's boundary, 0.035 apart, and out from 0.2 before the next, 0.02 apart, so in any rank one line has gone before the next one moves. Every chapter holds its text composed for 0.6 screens or more; the first rises with the stage and the last never leaves. The contents (four bars, numbered and named on wide screens) mark the chapter being told and fill as it is read (`storyChapter`, `storyFill`). Each change to the drawing starts as its chapter's text hands over, so word and picture turn together.
- **The drawing.** The lines are traced from the rendering (`src/content/story-drawing.ts`; "The story drawing" in IMAGES.md), so each has two places: over the picture and on the sheet. Strokes are hairlines at any size (`vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`) and lines of one sort are the subpaths of one path, so a frame rewrites nine paths, never two hundred, and only while they change. Bubbles, datums and labels keep their screen size; on sheets under 560px wide the level labels shorten to L1 and L2. The caption says the picture is a rendering with linework drawn over it for illustration: nothing on the sheet is a measurement of the project.
- **Layouts.** Landscape screens set the sheet on the left, at most 72% of the screen high, its title block down its right side, with the label, the contents and the chapter's text in a column beside it. Portrait screens stack the label, the sheet, the contents and the text in one screen. There the sheet is fitted to the room the other bands leave it, whatever their lines, through a size container (`.story-frame`): as wide as the gutters allow, or as wide as that room's height allows, sitting on its caption. Its title block is one row of one-line cells, so its height is known, and a container query on the sheet shows only the fields its width has room for (from three on phones to five on wide portrait tablets). Phones set the bodies smaller (0.875rem), screens under 800px tall drop the label's note, and screens under 620px tall (an iPhone SE in Safari) drop the chapters' bodies, so the drawing keeps a readable size; the titles carry the story there.
- **Still.** Without the tracks the chapters are a list beside a still sheet (below it on phones): the sketch over the left half of the house and the rendering on the right, meeting at a hairline, with the title block filled in.

### The making of a house

One client house, the big contemporary house the homepage opens on, followed from its site to its permit set, between Selected projects and the values. `e` is screens of scroll since the stage pinned; the chapters start at 0, 1.15, 2.45, 4.2 and 5.45, and the stage releases at 7.2 (`MAKING`).

- **The board** (`src/content/making-drawing.ts`; "The making-of drawings" in IMAGES.md) is the 16:9 crop of the front rendering, 3547 by 1995 units, so every drawing on it lands on the rendering's own pixels. It holds, bottom to top: the rendering by day, at dusk, and the dusk rendering's glass alone; a paper veil; the site plan; the floor plan; the elevation; the structure; the sheet's border; and the title block (HTML).
- **Site** (from −0.42). As the stage comes up the lot, then the setbacks, trees and pool, then the drive, walk and street are drafted line by line, each line run a little past its ends (`storyLineDraw`, `MAKING_OVERSHOOT`). The house's footprint fills in and the labels come up.
- **Plans** (from 1.15). The site is drawn in 2.4 times (`MK_SITE_ZOOM`, `makingZoom`, 0.38 screens on `power2InOut`) as it fades, so its house lands on the floor plan, whose walls, openings, doors and fittings are drafted over it; then the walls' poche and the rooms' names come up.
- **Design** (from 2.45). The plan gives way to the front elevation, drafted from the ground line up: outline, panels, openings, mullions, joints. The lit glass comes up in its windows, then the whole rendering by day, and the lines fade away.
- **Engineering** (from 4.2). A paper veil comes over the rendering with the elevation at half strength, and the structure is drafted in the brand red as on a structural overlay: a steel beam or a built-up header over every opening of the front plane, past both jambs; steel posts, hidden in the piers, under the long beams; the second floor's framing, broken where the house is two storeys high inside; and strip footings under the bearing walls with pads under the posts, below grade and dashed. Callouts in the sky and under the ground name them, each leader plumb over what it names.
- **Permit** (from 5.45). The sheet's double border is drafted round the board and the title block comes up: the firm, the project, the set and "For building permit". At 6.3 the drawings, veil, lit glass and title leave and the house is shown at dusk until the stage releases. Once the house is at dusk (`MAKING_FILM_AT`) its film plays once (`[data-mk-film]`): eight seconds of a slow push in, animated from the dusk rendering, whose first frame is that rendering, so it takes over without a cut. It shows only once it is really playing, over the still, and scrolled back it stops and rests at its first frame. H.264 at 1080p from 1024px wide and 720p below, with VP9 WebM copies for browsers without H.264; muted, inline, and loaded only when it first plays (`preload="none"`). Print and the still layouts never show it.
- **Text.** On the story's rhythm (`makingLine`): each chapter is composed for 0.6 screens or more, the first rises with the stage and the last never leaves, and the contents mark the chapter and fill as it is read (`makingChapter`, `makingFill`).
- **Cost.** A drawing's path is rewritten only while it is drafted; a layer is a layer of its own (`will-change`) only while it fades or zooms.
- **Layouts.** As the story and Works: landscape screens set the label, contents and text in a column with the board beside it; portrait screens stack the label, the board, the contents (five bars) and the text. The board is fitted 16:9 inside its frame (a size container). When the board is under 34rem wide the site and plan labels hide, the structure's callouts switch to a narrow layout in type about 9px tall on a phone (`mkCallouts` in `src/content/making-drawing.ts`), and the title block compacts.
- **Touch screens.** A layer fading, the site drawn in part way and a line cut by its mask are part way (`makingPartWay`); a drawing being drafted is whole. The page can rest about 0.65 screens on the site, 0.69 on the plan, 0.64 on the rendering by day, 0.65 on the structure, 0.52 on the permit set and 0.65 on the house at dusk (`tests/settle.test.ts`).
- **Still.** Without the tracks the chapters are a list beside the house at dusk.

### Services

- **Pinned where it fits.** Screens 768px wide or wider and 720px tall or taller get the 300vh track with the whole composition pinned for two screens: the two groups' labels and rows beside the frame, and under the frame the shown service's scope and the picture's note. The stage's sizes are capped by the screen's height, so the eyebrow to the "All services" link fits in about 690px at 720 tall. On portrait tablets the picture is never taller than its 4:5 at the column's width.
- **Entrance.** The stage rides 0.3 screen ahead of its track until sso 0.4, so the picture follows close under the last Values picture, then settles into the pin on `power2In`, its speed reaching 0 as it pins (`servicesLead`). The picture's sheet wipes up from the bottom of the screen as the frame comes into view and the picture settles from 120% (`servicesReveal`, `riseInset`). Once the ground is past half night the eyebrow (sso 0.34), the heading's lines (from 0.36, 0.06 apart) and then the list rise out of their masks: each group's label and then its rows, nine slots from 0.44, 0.03 apart (`servicesRow`), each row's rule drawing across. Each row's arrow (and, after the last row, "All services") is switched on once the name is most of the way up (`data-services-in`) and fades in on its own over 0.3s, a CSS transition rather than a value written every frame; the active row's dot waits for its row the same way. The last row is in place at sso 0.98, so the composition is whole for the whole hold. Only transforms, opacity and clip-path are written.
- **Hold.** Scrolling walks the rows: the hold is shared evenly between them (`servicesIndex`). The frame shows the row hovered (with a fine pointer), else the one focused, else the one reached by scrolling; a new scroll position takes over from a resting pointer. A hover is taken only from the pointer moving, and on the pinned track only while the stage holds (`servicesHold`), so rows rising or scrolling under a resting pointer never pick a picture. While the page is held (a page transition, the menu, the preloader) the frame keeps its picture: a click on a row moves focus and the transition's cover passes under the pointer, and neither swaps away the picture just chosen. Each new picture wipes up over the last (0.7s), which darkens under it, and the caption under it cross-fades to that service's scope and picture note (0.4s). The pictures are stacked layers (`swapTo`, `swapSettle`): a quick run over the rows never cuts from one picture to another, and going back to a picture still in the stack slides the sheets above it back down. The active row carries a small red dot hung before its name and an arrow drawn in at its end; the other names dim to half only while one row is singled out.
- **Keyboard.** Tabbing into the list from outside while the stage is not pinned scrolls to that row's place in the hold, where the list is whole. Focus inside the stage never moves the page, and moving on to "All services" keeps the last row's picture.
- **Phones** never pin it: there the section is one column that flows, too long to hold (about 2.6 screens at 390×844). The heading rises with the track as above, as the ground passes night. Each group's label, and each row (its name rising from its mask, its rule drawing across, its scope and arrow fading in behind), arrives from its own place as it comes up (sso 0.06 to 0.28 of its own top). Each group's picture wipes up from the bottom of the screen as it comes into view and settles from 120%, its note fading in with it (`servicesReveal`, `riseInset`). On a touch screen the labels, rows and pictures play in on their own clocks once they get there (see "Settle"). There is no frame, so no row is active.
- **Wide screens too short to pin** (under 720px tall): the section flows, starting close under Values. The heading rises with the track as above, each label and row arrives from its own place, and the picture follows the row crossing the middle of the screen.

### Services to Contact

Contact's ivory sheet rises over Services, which lags behind the scroll by up to 0.35 screen and dims to 0.45 as it is covered (`servicesHandover`). ### Contact

Contact is a quiet close, under one screen tall at 1440×900: an invitation beside one picture, then the firm's details set as the title block of a drawing sheet, the block the story's sheet is issued with. Each part runs from its own place on the screen (its own sso), so the order holds at every width.

- **Invitation.** The label, the heading's lines and the paragraph's lines rise from their masks, each line 0.04 after the one above it, over 0.3 (`contactLine`), so each block's lines are in place by about the time its top is halfway up the screen. The actions ("Start a project", and the phone number) rise 24px and fade in after them (`contactRise`).
- **Picture.** Its sheet wipes up from the bottom of the screen and the picture settles from 112% (`contactPicture`, `riseInset`); the caption fades in with it.
- **Title block.** The block is drawn in from the left over 0.24 (`contactBlock`, a `clip-path` wipe), and its cells come in behind the drawing's edge, rising 12px, left to right along each row, 0.02 apart (`contactCell`). It sits low in the section, so everything in it is done by its sso 0.38: at 1440×900 the block is whole as the section's top reaches the top of the screen. The cells are the firm's mark (PermitGuys, Architects), the office, the hours, phone and email, the Google rating and the credentials: one row from 1440px wide, three to a row from 768px, and rows of their own on phones, where the mark is left out (the footer's logo follows straight after).
- On a touch screen each part plays in on its own clock once it comes up (see "Settle"), in the same order.
- Whatever holds keyboard focus (the actions, a link in the block) is in place, whatever the scroll.

### The footer

On every page the footer's content rises out from under the sheet above it, lagging by up to 0.3 of its height (or of a screen) and in place once it is all on screen or the page ends (`footerProgress`, `footerLift`). The footer's own call to action stands down on pages that already close on one (the homepage's Contact, an inner page's `CtaPanel`, the contact form).

## Navigation and page transitions

- **Header** (`MainNav.tsx`): past 80px of scroll on screens 768px and wider, the top row leaves right to left (Contact first, 30ms apart, 0.28s, `--o`) and only then does the menu button rise into the corner (0.5s from 0.26s, once Contact's fade is all but done); coming back above 80px the button drops first (0.2s) and the items return left to right. Phones keep the logo and the menu button. The header reads the ground 64px down: ivory type over the hero, the night ground, dark sections and the footer, ink elsewhere. A dark section counts only until the next one begins, so a sheet sliding over it turns the header at once. Every tone change takes 0.3s: the bar, the type, the menu button and the logo, whose two versions share one grid cell and cross-fade. The menu button carries a 1px ring of its own colour, so it never merges with the ground. On phones the collapsed bar over the homepage hero is only a dark gradient that fades to nothing (no slab across the picture), on a layer of its own that fades in as the bar's ivory fades out, so the crossing never shows a grey band; light bars carry a hairline under them. On the homepage the phone header bar takes the ground's colour (`headerGround`), ivory as soon as Contact's sheet reaches it. Without JavaScript the header sits at the top of the page in the tone of the ground it starts on, and scrolls away with it.
- **Menu**: unfolds from the button's own outline (0.42s, `power2InOut`, opaque until near the end), the links rising one after another (0.5s from 0.15s, 30ms apart). Closing plays the same animations backwards from wherever they are (links three times as fast, the panel 1.35 times), so a quick second press reverses rather than snapping. On phones it fills the screen with the call to action at its foot (a short phone drops the line about the studio to keep it on screen); wider, it is a panel in the corner over a light scrim. The content scrolls inside the panel when it must, fading out under the logo and the close button, and at its foot too while there is more below (`data-more`), so the last line showing reads as more to come rather than cut off. Escape, a click outside (on the scrim) and navigation close it. Focus is trapped while it is open and returned to the button; each link's rising mask lets 4px past its edge, where the link's focus ring sits (1px out), so the mask never clips it.
- **Roll links**: header, menu and footer links roll their characters (0.4s, 0.02s apart) on hover and keyboard focus, and roll back from wherever they have got to.
- **Hero depth** (`HomeMotion.tsx`, hero module; `HERO_DEPTH`): with a fine pointer on the tracks every plane of the hero is drawn 4% larger (`--z`), and the house moves against the pointer, up to 1.5% of the width and 0.8% of the height, while the sky moves with it (0.9% and 0.5%). The planes follow on an exponential approach (rate 5 a second), settle back to the middle when the pointer leaves the window, and hold still once the intro covers the stage. Touch screens, reduced motion and short screens keep the planes still (and drawn at their own size); phones still get the planes' different depths on scroll.
- **Link buttons** (`LinkButton.tsx`, `wipe.ts`): on hover and keyboard focus a darker sheet of the plate's own colour wipes across the label (0.45s, `--e-sheet`; charcoal on ink, stone on ivory) and leaves forwards, through the far edge. The fill is one band (`--lb-a` to `--lb-b`) that both the wipe and the outline tones' second label are clipped to, and each change runs the band on from wherever it is (in a time in proportion to the way it has to go), so a pointer passing quickly over a button leaves a sliver that runs out ahead of it, and a pointer coming back while the fill leaves turns it round: nothing ever jumps. Without JavaScript the fill simply runs back the way it came; a highlight passes over the label (0.6s); just after (0.06s), the icon cell turns a quarter on its cube (0.45s, `--e-out-strong`) to a face whose arrow is the button's one red; both cells round their corners by 2px. The outline tone (on paper) and the ghost tone (on dark grounds) are a 1px ring that fills with ink or ivory, the label changing colour exactly where the fill has reached. The contact form's submit and the map card use the same face (`LinkButtonFace`); the form's shows a spinner while sending. In print a link button is an ink outline with an ink label. FAQ toggles, related-link rows and the footer's call to action use the square cell on its own, the cube turning as their row is hovered or focused (the footer's is the one red square, 3.5rem). Focus rings sit 4px out, brand-300 on dark grounds.
- **Page transitions** (`ScreenTransition.tsx`): on a click to another page the link dims at once and a dim underlay fades in (0.25s); a dark grey sheet (`#121212`, as the intro) rises from the bottom edge to cover the screen (0.6s, `--e-sheet`) while the intro's Greater Toronto Area map draws on it in white line (`GtaOutline.tsx`: the outline from 0.15s over 0.55s, then the borders between the regions, 0.05s apart, 0.45s each), under it the label GREATER TORONTO AREA, whose red dot pops as the last line closes; the five regions' pictures (the small and middle cuts) fade up west to east from 0.4s, 0.07s apart, 0.37s each, settling from 106%, so the map is complete by about 1.05s. Only then does the router change the page, so its commit never stalls a line. Once the new page is laid out and its opening picture decoded (at most 0.9s), focus moves to its main content and the sheet leaves through the top (0.6s, `power2InOut`, which gets going sooner than `--e-sheet`), the dimming clearing with it (0.3s); the outline stays on the sheet, drifting up 16px, until the sheet's edge clears it, so the exit never opens on a plain screen. The pressed link lets go as the cover lifts, however the navigation ended; the page's entrance runs on the route timing (`data-arrival="route"`). Each navigation has an id, so a late timer from an earlier click can never lift the cover over the wrong page, and a click while the cover is lifting turns it back. With reduced motion it is a 0.3s crossfade.
- **History and in-page links**: every page's scroll position is kept by URL (path, query and fragment, for the session). Back and forward cover the screen with a quick fade (0.28s), hold the router until it is covered, return the page to the pixel the reader left and lift the cover (0.32s); entrances and reveals do not replay on the way back (`data-returning`). A link to the page already showing glides to its top and closes the menu; a link that only changes the query (the contact form's preselected service) does the same and lets the router swap the page in place, keeping the form's choice, and the position it left is kept under its own URL for Back. From the keyboard, either moves focus to the page's main content, as a navigation does (wide screens put the menu button away at the top). In-page links ("On this page", the skip link) glide to their section (1.1s, the wheel's exponential ease; a jump with reduced motion), add a history step, and then move focus to the section; their heading lands level with the sticky index. Any glide waits for the menu to release the page first. Nothing the reader has passed is left blank: a sweep every tenth frame, when the page has moved since the last one, shows any block already above the screen, and stops once nothing is left hidden.
- **Inner pages** (`InteractionEffects.tsx`, `reveal.ts`): the page header enters in order once the preloader or transition clears; below it headings rise line by line, and rows, rules and blocks follow one another in reading order (60ms apart, at most 480ms) as they reach the bottom 8% of the screen. Anything that takes focus or the pointer shows at once.
- **Parallax** (`MotionRoot.tsx`): pictures marked `data-parallax` sit at 120% and drift through a tenth of their height as they cross the screen.

## Reduced motion and still layouts

With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`: Lenis duration 0 and no wheel smoothing; no intro screen at all (the page is simply there); every homepage section shows its still layout with all content visible; the Services picture follows a hover or focus at once; Contact's lines, picture and title block are simply there; roll links, parallax and the inner-page reveals are off; page transitions and Back and forward are a 0.3s crossfade, and glides are jumps; CSS durations collapse to 0.01ms.

Screens under 500px tall (a phone on its side) and visitors without JavaScript get the same still layouts, with motion otherwise allowed. So does a printed page, from wherever it was scrolled to: the tracks CSS applies to screens only, and the engine turns the tracks off from `beforeprint` to `afterprint`, so every module clears what it wrote before the page prints and writes it again after. In print a project or a value stays on one sheet with its picture, the making of a house shares one with its label and chapters, dark grounds keep their graphite, and sticky boxes print in the flow. Every split line keeps its text for screen readers once.

## Where this differs from the spec

- **Choreography.** The spec's section behaviours were replaced section by section: the hero is covered by the intro's sheet rather than fading, the orbit is one squircle path rather than seven orbit slots, Works is one pinned viewer with wipes rather than resolving panels, Values is an odometer with a union mask, Services pins beside its list on wide screens and flows on phones, and Contact is a sheet that closes on the firm's title block. The story, one house drawn from sketch to permit sheet, and the making of a house, one client house from its site to its permit set, are new. Display type never fades.
- **No WebGL layer.** Its shaders were never captured, so nothing of them is guessed. The `homeCard` FLIP between a works card and its page belongs to that layer and is not built either.
- **Colours** are PermitGuys' ivory (`#f4f1eb`), stone and graphite (`#191918`), and the ground goes through dusk into night for Services.
- **The intro** draws the Greater Toronto Area's five regions and fills them with client renderings rather than showing the arch logo; the transition panel carries the map in line, and the footer the logo.
- **Link button corners** round by 2px with a CSS `clip-path` rather than the reference's hand-built path morph, and the buttons stay square-cornered at rest, in the brand's ink, ivory and stone; red is only the turned arrow and the footer's one square.
- **Short screens** get the still layout rather than the reference's short-landscape variant.

## What could not be verified

- **Real devices.** Checked in headless Chromium at 1440×900, 1280×720, 1280×680, 1024×720, 820×1180, 390×844, 375×700, 360×740, 360×620, 320 wide and 844×390 (the story also at the heights phones give a page in Safari with its bars showing: 375×553, 390×664, 375×635 and 430×750, and at 768×960 and 820×1100; the making of a house at 1440×900 and 390×844, and its still layout), with frame captures, wheel recordings forward and back, and traces at four times CPU throttle; not on a physical phone or in Safari or Firefox (the iris needs registered custom properties; others get the fade).
- **Frame cost.** Through the dusk at four times CPU throttle the median frame takes about 30ms of main thread and the slowest about 57ms; unthrottled, frames there stay near 9 to 10ms. Through the story at four times throttle the median frame takes about 18ms and the slowest 45 to 65ms (where the sheet rises during the intro's fling, where the trace wipes up, and as the drawing straightens); unthrottled the median is about 4ms and all but one or two frames stay under 16ms. Per frame the time is mostly the scroll update, the style recalculation of the elements being moved, and paint (the clip-path wipes repaint their pictures, and layers at the edge of the area Chrome keeps painted repaint as the page scrolls). The root's night ground is no longer read by any style rule, so turning it on and off (as the dusk falls, and as Contact reaches the header) no longer restyles the whole document, which had cost a 20ms frame each time. In this environment's software rendering the last screen before the footer still shows a few 16 to 27ms frames while the compositor rasterises; not measured on real hardware or a low-end phone.
- **The hero's frame cost.** Each plane is a layer of its own that is only moved and scaled (`will-change: transform`), and the house is a transparent picture of its own rather than the rendering under a CSS mask, so scrolling through the hero and moving the mouse over it redraw neither picture. At four times CPU throttle at 1440×900 with a device pixel ratio of 2, scrolling through the hero went from 64 frames over 25ms (and 7.0s of rasterising) to 2 (0.17s), and moving the mouse from 4 such frames to none; on a 390×844 phone the rasterising went from 1.6s to 0.04s. On a slow connection the pictures arriving after the page transition no longer stall a frame (250ms before they were decoded first). In this environment's software compositing the opening's settle draws about 15ms a frame at a ratio of 2 (about 11ms with the mask, and the same as before at a ratio of 1), as it blends the house's transparent layer over the sky in software; a GPU compositor blends it for next to nothing, but this was not measured on real hardware.
- **Phones.** At 390×844 with a device pixel ratio of 3 and four times CPU throttle, against the version before (each measured twice, side by side): while the outline draws on a first load, its longest stall went from 400–650ms to 170–200ms (what remains is the page's script starting up); a thumb scrolling the whole homepage met 53 frames over 25ms instead of 103, one over 50ms instead of five, and the slowest took 83ms instead of 217ms; on a page change every long frame now falls while the cover is still, none as it lifts. At 1440×900 at the same throttle the opening gained as much, and wheeling from the story to Contact met 11 frames over 25ms instead of 42. Screenshots at 50 points down the homepage match the version before to the pixel, but for sub-pixel edges. Playwright's own listeners make some touches wait in both versions, so the touch figures understate the gain: the site itself keeps no listener that can hold a touch while the page scrolls.
- **Settling.** A thumb's flicks down the whole homepage in headless Chromium (eight touch moves, then Chrome's own fling), gentle, medium and hard at 390×844 and medium at 375×667, 430×932 and 820×1180, came to rest with nothing part way every time: about 760 rests over ten runs (before: 39 of 78 rests at 390×844). With the making of a house added, medium and hard flicks at 390×844 came to rest 121 times with nothing part way (the veil and the elevation's lines rest at their set strengths over the structure). Not tried on a physical phone: iOS Safari's momentum and its toolbar may behave differently, and how the glide feels under a real thumb is a judgement only a phone can give.
- **Sleep and settle, frame cost.** At 390×844 with a device pixel ratio of 3 and four times CPU throttle, a thumb scrolling the whole homepage against the version before (each measured twice, side by side): the script run in each frame went from 7.1–7.3s in all to 5.2–5.5s, and the main thread's work from the hero to the end of Values from 33–34s to 30–31s. Services, Contact and the footer take 2.5–3s more, as their entrances now play their full 0.9 to 1.6s after each flick rather than only while the thumb moves; frame for frame they cost the same. Frames over 50ms are unchanged (three or four, the slowest 83–100ms against 83–117ms), and so are frames over 25ms, within the noise (174–236 against 180–254). Screenshots at 51 points down the homepage at 1440×900, and at 53 in a 390px window with a mouse, match the version before to the pixel.
- **Line counts** depend on the fonts actually loaded; a split made before the fonts arrive is made again once they do, and splitting re-runs whenever a block's width changes.
- **The reference.** Only a still frame of Sebra's video arrived and the live reference site cannot be opened from the build environment.
